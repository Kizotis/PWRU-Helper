> **The largest deliberate traffic in the whole plan: ~2 requests/second for ≥ 2 hours, on ≥ 2 networks.** That is

## Story

As the owner,
I want to know whether the new endpoint survives an evening, not twenty requests,
So that "the switch works" is a measurement rather than a sample of one afternoon.

## Acceptance Criteria

_Copied verbatim from `epics.md` § Story E3.S9. Do not weaken._

1. **Given** U3 and V1.7
   **When** an instrumented branch build issues ~2 requests/second against
   `clients5.google.com/translate_a/t?client=dict-chrome-ex` for **≥ 2 hours**, on **≥ 2 different networks**
   **Then** the per-request log (E1.S5) and the gate transition log (E2.S6) are archived in this folder with the
   network, the date and the address family.
2. **Exit criterion (measurable).** Number of 429s per hour, the strike level reached, and the total requests the
   gate absorbed versus the total the soak attempted.
3. **Go / no-go.** **Go:** zero 429s, or 429s the gate absorbs into a handful of probes ⇒ the switch is worth
   what `benchmark…` §3.2 claims. **No-go:** sustained 429s at 2 req/s ⇒ the endpoint is not a relief, R1 has
   already fired, and the finding goes to the owner **before** the E2+E3 release ships.
4. **Given** the incident of `SYNTHESE.md` §5
   **When** the soak is scheduled
   **Then** it runs from a network the owner nominates and **never** from the address recorded as 429 on
   2026-09-06 until it has been confirmed clear.

## Dev Notes

### This story writes no production code

Branch only. Its deliverables are two archived logs, four numbers and a closed §15.1 row.

### Why it is worth ~29,000 requests

`benchmark…` §3.2's evidence is **20 sequential requests at ~2.5 req/s**, once, on one afternoon, from one
network. That is enough to justify the switch as a *relief*; it is not enough to say what the endpoint does over
an evening of LIVE. `gtx` itself was fine for two years and then 429'd on request #1 from a clean IP, with a dense
cluster of independent projects reporting the same break from ~2026-08-22 (`benchmark…` §5.1). U3 is the
difference between "it worked when we tried it" and "we know what it does".

### The soak is also a test of E2

AC 2's fourth number — *requests the gate absorbed* — is the first field measurement of the whole of Epic 2. If
the gate absorbs a burst of 429s into a handful of probes, E2 works. If it opens and never closes, **R-01** has
fired and that is a bigger finding than U3's own. Watch for it explicitly.

### It feeds E2.S7

`e2-s7` (U9 — tune the gate windows from field logs) is scheduled **after** the A.1 release and is the story that
turns `OpenBaseSeconds` / `OpenCapMinutes` / `CleanResetMinutes` / `SoftCooldownSecs` from [ASSUMED] into
measured. This soak is the first real dataset it will have. Archive the logs in a form E2.S7 can use, not just
read.

### Repo traps

- **`ProviderGates.PathOverride` and the log path must be redirected** for the run, or a 2-hour soak writes a
  large `provider-state.json` churn and a large `log.txt` into the developer's real `%AppData%` — the trap
  `LoggingTests` and `ProviderStateStoreTests` already guard the *suite* against.
- **`LogSuppressor`** (E1.S5's storm valve) exists precisely to stop a failure storm from filling the log. In a
  soak that is *counting* failures, a suppressed line is a lost measurement — check whether it engages and, if it
  does, record how many lines it swallowed rather than turning it off silently.
- **No production file changes.** If the harness needs a hook the app does not have, that is a finding to record,
  not a reason to edit `Services/`.

## Testing
